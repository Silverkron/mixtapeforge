## Prepare before touching anything

Use your model's cleaning instructions, a light, lint-free swabs and only the cleaning fluid approved for each part. The **head** reads/writes the tape; the **capstan** is the rotating metal shaft controlling its movement; the **pinch roller** presses the tape against it. Metal and rubber may need different treatment. Do not assume alcohol approved for heads is approved for the roller.

1. Remove the cassette, switch off and unplug the deck. Follow the manual for exposing the accessible path. Do not defeat door interlocks or touch a powered moving mechanism.
2. Inspect the head face, capstan and roller. Note visible residue, loose fibres, cracks or sticky rubber. Take a photo if it helps compare later.
3. Lightly moisten a swab with the approved cleaner; it must not drip. Wipe the accessible head face gently, avoiding screws and surrounding wiring.
4. Clean the accessible capstan surface and roller with their prescribed method. Do not force the mechanism to expose hidden surfaces or flood bearings. If access requires disassembly, leave that part to service.
5. Remove loose fibres, allow everything to dry fully as the cleaner/manual requires, then reconnect power.
6. Test a disposable, known-good cassette at low listening volume. Stop immediately if tape motion becomes irregular or the machine starts [pulling out tape](../tape-eating/).

## Judge the result

Clearer, repeatable playback after cleaning supports a contact-related contribution; it does not certify alignment. If rubber is cracked, glazed or sticky, or the same symptom returns immediately, seek service rather than applying more solvent. Demagnetizing is a separate, model-dependent procedure and is not part of this routine.

Before [calibration](../calibration-basics/), confirm the tape travels normally and repeated playback sounds consistent. Otherwise the measurements may describe an unstable dirty mechanism rather than a correction worth saving.
