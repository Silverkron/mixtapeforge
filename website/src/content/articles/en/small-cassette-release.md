## Choose home recording or a duplicator

For a few home-made copies, reserve real-time recording and checking time. MixtapeForge can arrange sides and export WAV masters; it does not operate a duplication plant or print shell labels.

A manufacturer may require one continuous file per side, a specific sample format and its own artwork template. National Audio Company currently requests program-ready side files at 44.1 kHz/16-bit and a matching tracklist. That is its delivery requirement, not a universal quality target. Confirm the chosen supplier’s current instructions before export.

## Prepare a release folder

1. Finalize A and B with intentional gaps. Export the approved sides and listen to the actual exported files from beginning to end.
2. Export the tracklist and check names, order and durations against those files. Use clear filenames such as Artist_Title_A and Artist_Title_B.
3. Select [the real packaging format](../release-packaging/) and use its template. Add artist, title, side information and credits; [record artwork permissions](../artwork-credits/).
4. Make one cassette and one physical cover proof. For outsourced work, ask for the supplier’s proof process and resolve discrepancies before approving the run.
5. Freeze that version. Keep approved files, project, permissions and production notes together. Use [the copy-check workflow](../consistent-copies/) for home duplication.

## Decide what counts as acceptable

Listen for missing beginnings/endings, swapped sides, channel loss and unexpectedly distorted passages. Read every track through the case and check it closes. These checks are more useful than approving colour on a monitor alone.

**Release-ready:** masters heard completely, tracklist matches, proof approved, intended use of music/artwork cleared, copies traceable to one version. Keep a finished reference copy for future reorders.
