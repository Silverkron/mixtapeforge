A piano note that bends or a held guitar chord that shimmers can reveal changing tape speed. Slow pitch variation is often called **wow**; faster fluctuation is called **flutter**. A consistently sharp recording is a different [speed problem](../playback-speed-problems/).

## Separate tape, player and recording

Use a familiar cassette containing sustained notes, a second good cassette and headphones. Fresh batteries are useful for a portable. A recorded vibrato or chorus effect can sound similar, so check the original music where possible.

1. Listen to the same sustained passage twice. Note whether the fluctuation repeats at exactly the same musical point or changes.
2. Try the second tape. If both fluctuate, check power supply/batteries and any user pitch setting.
3. Inspect the accessible tape path with power disconnected. Follow [the model's cleaning method](../clean-cassette-deck/), allow full drying, then retest a disposable cassette.
4. If tape travel looks irregular, stop. Do not keep playing through a transport fault or attempt to pull tape while it moves.
5. Compare the suspect cassette on another working deck if available. A repeatable wobble there may already be recorded into the tape; only one player wobbling suggests its transport needs attention.

## Decide whether to measure or repair

If the machine cannot move tape safely, repair comes before measurement. If motion is safe but audibly unstable, a documented 3,000/3,150 Hz reference tape and a dedicated analyzer can quantify the symptom; use the [wow-and-flutter procedure](../wow-flutter/).

A percentage without its weighting and detector is incomplete. Those settings determine which fluctuations count and how peaks are summarized, so different methods can give different numbers. A music spectrum or a normal frequency counter alone is not the same measurement.

MixtapeForge's recording EQ profile cannot stabilize a motor or remove time-varying pitch error. Do not store such instability as a frequency-response correction; verify stable playback before calibrating the recording chain.
