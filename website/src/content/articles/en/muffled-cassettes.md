## A ten-minute listening check

Use headphones, one familiar good cassette and the suspect cassette. An optional second working deck makes comparison easier. Do not adjust head screws for this test.

1. Set tone controls flat and disable extra sound effects. Select the suspect tape's documented [playback EQ](../playback-equalization/) and NR mode.
2. Play a short passage, noting whether the left, right or both channels lack treble.
3. Play the known-good tape with its own correct settings. If it too is dull, follow the deck manual's [cleaning procedure](../clean-cassette-deck/), let the path dry and repeat once.
4. Replay the suspect passage. If its clarity changes between passes, stop treating this as a fixed EQ error: inconsistent tape contact or tape condition needs investigation.
5. If available, play the suspect cassette on the second deck with matching settings. Do not change its alignment to suit the tape.

## Follow the result

| Observation | Next useful action |
| --- | --- |
| Only the suspect tape is dull on both decks | Check its recording history and physical condition |
| All tapes are dull on one deck | Inspect that deck's playback path; seek service if cleaning/settings do not help |
| Only new recordings are dull | Compare the digital source, then record the same passage at a lower level |
| Dullness appears mainly with NR on | Follow [Dolby tracking checks](../dull-dolby/) |

If lowering the recording level restores bright percussion, [high-frequency saturation](../high-frequency-saturation/) is a useful next test. A global treble boost can worsen that problem by sending even more treble to the tape. Save correction profiles only after playback is stable and the same difference repeats; a profile cannot recover missing information or repair intermittent contact.
