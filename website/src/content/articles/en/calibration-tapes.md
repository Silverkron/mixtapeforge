## Buy for a particular measurement

| Task | Information the reference must provide |
| --- | --- |
| Playback speed | Nominal tone frequency, normally 3000 or 3150 Hz, plus recording accuracy |
| Wow and flutter | Suitable speed tone and sufficiently low, documented residual instability |
| Azimuth | Reference head alignment, suitable high-frequency tone and track format |
| Playback level | Frequency, magnetic fluxivity, equalization and tolerance |
| Playback response | Frequencies, recorded relative levels, equalization and corrections |

**Fluxivity**, expressed in nanowebers per metre (nWb/m), describes the magnetic reference recorded on tape. It is not a computer volume or voltage setting. **Equalization** describes the recording/playback frequency characteristic used by the reference. Both must suit the service procedure.

## Before ordering

1. Find your exact deck's service manual and the test-tape specification for the measurement you want.
2. Ask the supplier for the relevant frequency, level, equalization, track format, tolerance and reference method. “Professionally recorded” alone does not answer these questions.
3. Confirm that the product is a compact cassette at the correct speed. MRL's useful technical literature concerns magnetic recording, but MRL explicitly does not manufacture compact-cassette calibration tapes.
4. Inspect the supplied documentation and label it with the cassette. Keep it away from magnets and do not record onto it.
5. Before using an expensive reference, establish that the deck transports an ordinary cassette without creasing, eating or shedding tape.

## What a mismatch does

Hypothetical example: a 3150 Hz reference treated as 3000 Hz appears 5% fast even if playback is correct. That is a setup error, not a reason to adjust the motor.

A used cassette of uncertain history may still provide a useful comparison, but its label cannot prove that the original level or alignment survived. Repeated unstable readings call for another reference or professional verification before changing the deck.

You do not need every tape at once. Start with [what can be measured without one](../without-reference-tape/), then buy or borrow the reference needed for a [speed check](../speed-test/) or [playback-level comparison](../playback-level/). Do not use a good-looking round-trip graph as proof that an unverified reference is accurate.
