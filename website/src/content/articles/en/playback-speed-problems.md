A cassette running consistently too fast raises pitch and shortens the music; running too slowly lowers pitch and lengthens it. This is different from [pitch that wavers](../wobbly-playback/). Establish which symptom you have before looking for a motor adjustment.

## Basic checks without opening the player

You need a known recording, the player manual and, for a portable, fresh batteries or the specified power supply. A stopwatch and the original source help comparison, but do not replace a speed reference tape.

1. Check normal-speed playback and center any user pitch control. Do not compare a different edit, remaster or live version as though it were the same performance.
2. Try the same familiar cassette and a second good tape. Note whether both sound consistently sharp/flat or change pitch during playback.
3. On a battery player, repeat with fresh batteries. Check normal tape travel; stop if the cassette drags or tangles.
4. If available, compare the tape on another working deck. One tape wrong on both may have been recorded at the wrong speed; all tapes wrong on one player directs attention to that player.
5. For a real measurement, obtain a reference cassette with a documented 3,000 or 3,150 Hz tone and follow the [speed-test procedure](../speed-test/).

## Understand the measurement

If a certified 3,000 Hz reference plays at 3,030 Hz, the indicated speed error is +1%: `(3030 / 3000 − 1) × 100`. This is an illustrative calculation, not a measured result here. Use the nominal frequency printed for your tape; confusing 3,000 and 3,150 creates a large false error.

Recording and replaying your own tone on the same deck cannot establish absolute speed: similar record/play errors may cancel. Do not turn internal motor trimmers by ear. Once speed is corrected with the model's service procedure, verify [wow and flutter](../wow-flutter/) separately; correct average speed does not guarantee stable motion.
