## Give each side a reason to exist

Choose a few songs that connect to the person, then build around them. A possible idea is “the journey out” on A and “the way home” on B. This is an editorial example, not a rule: two moods, two decades or two shared places can work equally well.

Listen to transitions before filling the remaining minutes. One carefully chosen ending is worth more than an extra song cut in half. Use [the mixtape workflow](../cassette-mixtape/) and leave [time for gaps and endings](../cassette-duration/).

Ask whether their player supports the NR you intend to use. If its capabilities are unknown, recording without encoded NR avoids requiring a matching decoder; it also means accepting the tape’s hiss. Do not label a tape Dolby B unless it was actually encoded that way.

## Add a note they will use

Write a title, side order and playback instruction. A short personal sentence can explain the selection without annotating every song. Keep extensive memories on a separate note so the tracklist remains readable. MixtapeForge’s Cover screen provides a dedication/subtitle field, but it is not a booklet editor.

Before wrapping, play both beginnings and endings from the cassette, then one loud and one quiet passage. Rewind to side A’s start and make side labels unmistakable. Follow [the final listening checks](../before-sending-mixtape/).

A useful gift includes its own route back to the music: names of the songs, a clear A/B order and any required playback NR. The decoration should make that invitation easier to accept.
