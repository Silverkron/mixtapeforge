ANRS is JVC's analogue noise-reduction system. Like [Dolby B](../dolby-b/), it processes the signal for recording and requires corresponding playback processing. **Super ANRS adds complementary high-level treble processing**: compression during recording and expansion during playback. It is not HX Pro, and it is not an interchangeable name for ordinary ANRS.

## Use the controls your JVC actually provides

You need the exact model's manual, the cassette's recording information and headphones. JVC models do not all label or operate their switches identically. The [KD-A77 service manual](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1296270/Jvc-Kd-A77-A.html?page=3) identifies separate ANRS/Super ANRS indications; that is not a universal operating sequence.

1. Identify whether the cassette was recorded without NR, with ANRS or with Super ANRS. Do not infer this from the JVC brand alone.
2. Find the playback setting prescribed in that model's operating manual. Super ANRS needs the playback arrangement specified for that system; ordinary ANRS or Dolby B is not an exact substitute for its additional high-level processing.
3. Compare a quiet voice and cymbal decay with the documented mode selected. Match listening volume when comparing with NR off.
4. For a new recording, test a short passage on the destination player before making a complete side.

ANRS was intended for interchange with Dolby B, but Super ANRS recording treatment and aged machines make an actual listening test useful. A bright or dull result alone cannot prove the recording format. Consult [playback-mode identification](../dolby-b-c-s-playback/) if the notes are missing.

MixtapeForge does not provide an ANRS or Super ANRS mode. Do not label Type B output as verified Super ANRS. Keep the exact recording mode on the cover and use the [compatibility checklist](../playback-compatibility/) when sharing it.
