## What belongs on the cassette

Write the title, artist/track order for A and B, recording date if meaningful, and playback NR such as “Dolby B” or “NR off”. Use the setting actually used, not a label chosen because it looks vintage. If you are uncertain, resolve it before printing; [matching NR matters at playback](../dolby-b-c-s-playback/).

A dedication can be one sentence. Optional stories, source editions and lengthy credits can go on another sheet rather than competing with the songs. See [legible cover typography](../cover-typography/).

## Keep a recording log beside the project

After the short test succeeds, write down:

- Deck model, cassette brand/model/type and which side was recorded.
- Audio output device, physical output pair and volume settings; REC LEVEL position.
- Software processing/profile and whether NR encoding happened in software or in the deck.
- Any observed problem and the exact point where it occurred.

A knob position is a repeatability note, not a calibrated voltage. If you move a cable or change output volume, update the log and repeat the level check.

**Illustrative note:** “A/B recorded on 26 October; Type I; hardware Dolby B; output and REC LEVEL unchanged after test; check B’s final fade.” Replace this with your actual conditions.

MixtapeForge saves project settings and exports its tracklist from Cover. Keep a separate plain-text note for hardware positions and observations; do not assume the project knows them. This makes [later copies](../consistent-copies/) easier to reproduce without turning the insert into a service sheet.
