Playback equalization is the deck's standardized way of reading recorded tape. Choosing the wrong setting changes brightness even when the cassette is healthy. It is separate from the tone controls and from [noise reduction](../bias-eq-nr/).

## Choose 120 or 70 µs

The µs symbol means microseconds and identifies an EQ time constant. Ordinary Type I recordings use 120 µs; Type II and IV recordings normally use 70 µs. Some prerecorded chrome tapes specify 120 µs. Follow the recording information rather than assuming the tape material tells the whole story.

You need the cassette's label, the deck manual and a known recording for comparison. No special tone is needed simply to select the documented setting.

1. Find the label's playback EQ instruction. For a home recording, check the recorder's setting or notes.
2. On a manually switched deck, select the matching NORMAL/120 µs or HIGH/METAL/70 µs position as its manual defines it.
3. On an automatic deck, check which tape type it detects. If it cannot override detection for a special prerecorded tape, note the limitation; do not cut or cover sensing notches experimentally.
4. Select the matching Dolby mode separately and play a familiar voice and cymbal passage.

With a 120-µs recording played at 70 µs, the treble tends to be reduced; the reverse mismatch tends to brighten it. That tendency is useful for understanding the switch, not for proving an unknown tape's format.

## If it still sounds wrong

Check [muffled playback](../muffled-cassettes/) before assuming EQ is the cause. Dirt or head-contact problems can change between passes, whereas the EQ selection is repeatable. For digitization, document the actual playback setting. A software EQ converter can compensate a known curve mismatch, but a genre preset is not that conversion. Never apply a conversion twice.
