High Com Explained: Another Approach to Cassette Noise Reduction

Recognize a High Com recording and keep its playback requirements with the cassette.

Quick answer

Telefunken High Com is a compander: it reduces the difference between quiet and loud music for recording and expands it again on playback. Its action covers a broad frequency range, rather than mainly the treble region associated with Dolby B. The Telefunken RC100 manual explains this complementary process.

What a collector needs to check

A tape marked High Com needs matching decoding. High Com, High-Com II and professional telcom c4 must not be treated as interchangeable names. A Dolby or dbx switch does not substitute for the corresponding High Com decoder.

You need the cassette’s notes, the exact recorder/processor manual and working compatible playback equipment. No EQ preset can stand in for a missing expander.

  1. Identify the full format name from the recording notes or original equipment. If unknown, keep the uncertainty in your archive notes.
  2. For a deck with built-in High Com, use the playback mode specified by its manual.
  3. With an external decoder, connect deck LINE OUT L/R to the decoder’s playback input, then its output to an amplifier’s line input. Leave the deck’s other NR off. Follow the processor’s connection labels; do not use speaker terminals.
  4. Replay quiet and loud passages. Check that small details remain audible and the background does not surge unnaturally after notes.

Unnatural level changes suggest a format, signal-path or playback problem; they are not proof that the cassette needs EQ. Use compatibility checks before altering anything.

Software and preservation

MixtapeForge has no High Com stage, and this guide has no verified direct software replacement to recommend. The software NR overview distinguishes the formats that available tools actually document. If matching hardware is unavailable, keep a clean undecoded capture and its recording notes for later work; call it undecoded, not restored.
