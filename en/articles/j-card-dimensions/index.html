J-cards & printing

Cassette J-Card Dimensions: Panels, Folds and the Actual Case

A J-card is the folded insert inside a cassette case: front, narrow spine and short rear flap.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. Use the right measurements
  2. Check fit before artwork
← Back to articles
Quick answer

A J-card is the folded insert inside a cassette case: front, narrow spine and short rear flap. To make one that fits, distinguish the whole flat sheet from the front panel you see through the lid.

Use the right measurements

MixtapeForge’s current flat template is 104.8 × 101.6 mm. Across the 104.8 mm dimension, the front occupies 65.1 mm, the spine 12.7 mm and the remaining flap 27.0 mm. These are this template’s dimensions, not a universal guarantee for every case. Rotating a drawing changes which dimension is called width; do not swap front and overall size.

A printer’s trim line is where paper is cut. A fold line bends the paper. Bleed is background extending beyond the final cut so small cutting shifts do not reveal white edges. For outsourced printing, use the supplier’s exact template and bleed requirement; MixtapeForge’s home-print export is not automatically that supplier’s production file.

Check fit before artwork

  1. Obtain the intended case and either its supplier template or a ruler and an existing well-fitting insert.
  2. Print an outline at 100%, measure it, cut and fold. Mark front, spine and flap in pencil on this test only.
  3. Close the case gently. If the paper bows, check the panel dimension and fold location. If the flap hits internal posts, use the packaging-specific shape rather than forcing it.
  4. Keep this successful dummy for checking later print-scale problems.

Extra fold-out panels need a different packaging layout. Once fit is proven, follow the printing procedure.

Accepted: flat measurements correct, folds match case, no bowing or hinge pressure.
