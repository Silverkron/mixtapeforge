J-cards & printing

How to Fit a Long Tracklist on a Cassette J-Card

When a tracklist no longer fits, keep the song names readable and change the layout.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. Know the current limit
  2. Move the list into a suitable layout
  3. Avoid hidden omissions
← Back to articles
Quick answer

When a tracklist no longer fits, keep the song names readable and change the layout. Shrinking everything until it fits on screen usually produces an unusable printed insert.

Know the current limit

MixtapeForge’s built-in J-card layouts support 15 tracks in the entire project, not 15 per side. Print, PDF and SVG buttons are disabled above that limit. Long text can also be abbreviated within a smaller list. This is an artwork limit, not a limit on how many songs you can arrange or record.

Move the list into a suitable layout

  1. Finalize the A/B order, then use Export tracklist in Cover. This text export remains available when the graphic export is too full.
  2. Choose a supplier’s extra-panel J-card template or create a separate insert in Inkscape or another layout editor. Check dimensions and folds against the actual case.
  3. Put the main title on the front, one clear heading per side, and one consistent line structure for songs. Move optional notes to another panel before reducing type.
  4. If an artist repeats, an artist heading can save space without truncating every song name. Keep track numbering tied to the actual tape order.
  5. Print a full-size proof with the complete real list. Compare line by line with the exported text and read through the case.

Avoid hidden omissions

Ellipses, a missing final track or a title clipped at a fold are failures even if the page looks balanced. A readable font cannot recover text removed by a template. For many panels, check paper thickness and folding before producing copies.

Done: every track present on the correct side, full names where necessary, no overlap, comfortable physical-size reading.
