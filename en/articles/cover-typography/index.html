Cassette Cover Typography: Making Tracklists Easy to Read

A cassette cover succeeds when you can read the songs through the closed case.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. Choose a title font, then test real names
  2. Fix the cause of cramped text
← Back to articles
Quick answer

A cassette cover succeeds when you can read the songs through the closed case. Judge lettering on paper, not only in a zoomed preview.

Choose a title font, then test real names

MixtapeForge’s Cover → Title font offers Barlow, Barlow Bold, Barlow Condensed, IBM Plex Mono, Bebas Neue and Courier Prime, plus the style’s recommended choice. This changes the title treatment; it is not an unrestricted editor for every track’s font size.

  1. Use the real title, longest artist name and longest track name. Include accents and punctuation you actually need.
  2. Choose an expressive title font and keep the tracklist calm. Compare a condensed title with a regular one; do not stretch letters to squeeze them in.
  3. Check the spine separately: a title readable on the front can still be clipped when rotated.
  4. Export PDF, reopen it and print at actual size. Read the smallest entry under ordinary light through the case.

Fix the cause of cramped text

If a title is clipped, shorten the optional subtitle or try a different title font. If track names are abbreviated, changing the title font will not solve it: use a longer-tracklist layout. Built-in templates hold up to 15 tracks total.

The app’s PDF turns font shapes into outlines to preserve appearance. The SVG keeps editable text and includes fonts, but an external editor may handle embedded fonts differently; inspect it after opening. In Inkscape, keep your editable SVG before exporting a print PDF.

Check: real names intact, accents present, spine readable, printed small text clear. For a darker design, also test black-and-white contrast.

Once the text works, choose a rock, pop or synthwave layout around it.
