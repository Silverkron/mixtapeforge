J-cards & printing

Cassette J-Card Design Ideas: A Small Format with a Clear Identity

Choose a visual idea that helps someone recognize the cassette and read it.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. Three starting points in MixtapeForge
  2. Turn the idea into a cover
← Back to articles
Quick answer

Choose a visual idea that helps someone recognize the cassette and read it. A rock or synthwave style is a starting palette and motif, not a requirement to fill every panel with decoration.

Three starting points in MixtapeForge

  • Rock: try Garage or Backstage, a bold title and one strong accent. Let the title carry the energy; keep song names clear.
  • Pop: try Color club, then choose a light/dark contrast that survives printing. Use a short title so the shapes do not compete with several lines of text.
  • Synthwave: try Night drive with its recommended palette. Inspect the printed dark areas: a luminous screen can hide weak contrast on paper.

Other current styles include Classical study, B/W archive, B&W fanzines, Rear radio and Home recorded. There are nine built-in styles; each supports custom background, text and two accent colours.

Turn the idea into a cover

  1. Finish the A/B track order first, then open Cover and select a style.
  2. Choose its suggested palette before changing individual colours. Set the title and optional dedication using real content.
  3. Check the spine, long titles and tracklist. Choose a suitable title font without sacrificing small-text readability.
  4. Print a paper proof and view it inside the case. Keep one successful version before experimenting further.

For an original illustration or unusual panel arrangement, export editable SVG and continue in a vector editor. The built-in controls do not provide arbitrary image placement. Use cleared artwork with proper credits.

Good result: recognizable title, readable A/B list, coherent colours and a spine you can identify on a shelf.
