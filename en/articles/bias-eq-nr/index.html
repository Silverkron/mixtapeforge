Noise reduction

Bias, EQ and Noise Reduction: Three Different Jobs

Know which cassette control changes recording behavior, tonal balance or noise-reduction tracking.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. Three controls, three jobs
  2. Set up an ordinary new recording
← Back to articles
Quick answer

A cassette deck may offer BIAS, EQ and NR near one another, but they solve different problems. Choosing all three by ear until the tape sounds bright can hide a wrong setting.

Three controls, three jobs

  • Bias is an ultrasonic signal added during recording to help magnetize the tape more predictably. It is not an audible treble boost and does nothing to an already recorded cassette during playback.
  • Playback EQ is the standardized frequency response used to read the recording correctly. Common cassette settings are 120 µs and 70 µs; the number describes a time constant, not a delay you hear.
  • NR is noise reduction. Dolby B/C/S require the playback mode to match the recording process. NR off is correct for an unencoded tape.

The tape selector may combine bias and EQ choices automatically. Separate controls on another deck do not mean the settings should be copied by physical knob position.

Set up an ordinary new recording

Use the blank’s packaging and your deck manual. No analyzer is needed to identify the intended operating settings.

  1. Identify the tape type and select the corresponding recording position. Confirm automatic detection if the deck has no manual selector.
  2. Leave any fine-bias adjustment at the documented starting position. Use a measured bias procedure before changing it.
  3. Choose NR according to the playback machine: for hardware Dolby B recording, software NR stays off and the deck uses B at both stages.
  4. Record and replay a short familiar passage. Use the playback EQ specified for that recording, then assess clarity and distortion.

For an existing cassette, bias is irrelevant: start with playback EQ and the documented NR mode. HX Pro is also a recording function; it does not require a decoder. MixtapeForge’s audio EQ changes the source music and cannot replace the deck’s bias or standardized playback equalization.
