J-cards & printing

Choosing Paper for Cassette J-Cards: Folding, Finish and Your Printer

Choose paper that your printer can feed and your case can close around.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. Make a small material test
  2. Interpret the proof
← Back to articles
Quick answer

Choose paper that your printer can feed and your case can close around. Heavier paper can feel nicer, but its weight in g/m² does not by itself tell you its thickness or folding behaviour.

Make a small material test

You need the printer’s media specification, two candidate papers, the actual case and a finished correctly scaled design. Start with ordinary paper to separate size problems from material problems.

  1. Check that the stock is approved for inkjet, laser or both. Laser heat and inkjet coatings are different; use the paper’s packaging and your printer’s manual.
  2. Check the permitted weight/thickness and tray. Do not assume a printer that accepts plain A4 also accepts heavy card through its duplex path.
  3. Print one copy with the matching media setting. Let it dry or cool as specified by the paper/printer manufacturer.
  4. Cut and fold, then insert it. Read fine text, look for ink smearing, cracks along folds and visible printing from the reverse.
  5. Repeat with the second paper, keeping the design unchanged. Pick the lighter stock if both look good but the thicker one makes the case tight.

Interpret the proof

A smeared image may indicate unsuitable coating or drying, not poor artwork. A white cracked fold suggests the stock or heavy ink/toner coverage is unsuitable for that fold. Try another stock or a lighter design such as a monochrome cover.

For double-sided work, test both sides: a nice front does not prove the reverse coating is printable.

Keep: paper name, media setting and a folded proof. Buy a large pack only after the case closes freely and the print remains clean.
