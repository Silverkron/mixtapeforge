Noise reduction

DNL and DNR: Noise Reduction Without a Recording Encoder

Understand playback-only noise reduction and the difference between quieter sound and faithful decoding.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide

  2. Try it without losing the original
← Back to articles
Quick answer

DNL and DNR are playback noise-reduction approaches that do not require the cassette to have been specially encoded. They reduce audible high-frequency noise by changing the playback response according to the signal. They must not be confused with a matching Dolby decoder.

DNL and DNR are playback noise-reduction approaches that do not require the cassette to have been specially encoded. They reduce audible high-frequency noise by changing the playback response according to the signal. They must not be confused with a matching Dolby decoder.

Hear the trade-off

Imagine a quiet cymbal fading into hiss. Restricting the high-frequency range can make the hiss less noticeable, but it may also shorten the apparent cymbal decay. The aim is a useful listening compromise, not recovery of information already lost.

Texas Instruments documents DNR in the LM1894 data sheet as a non-complementary system: no preceding encoder is needed. DNL is a separate implementation; do not assume identical controls or performance from the similar names.

Try it without losing the original

You need a player with the documented DNL/DNR function and a familiar cassette. For software restoration, keep an untouched capture and work on a copy.

  1. Select the cassette’s correct playback EQ and, if applicable, its proper Dolby decoder first.
  2. Play a quiet voice or acoustic passage with DNL/DNR off. Notice the consonants and reverberation tails, not just pauses.
  3. Enable the documented noise-limiting function and repeat the same passage at similar volume.
  4. If adjustable, choose the least processing that helps. If only an on/off switch exists, keep it off when detail loss outweighs the benefit.

A quieter pause accompanied by missing musical detail is a trade-off, not better fidelity. If noise rises and falls distractingly, reduce processing or bypass it.

MixtapeForge has no DNL/DNR mode. Modern denoisers can serve a related listening purpose but are not automatically emulations of these circuits; NR software and decoding explains the difference. Keep decoder mode, optional denoising and listening preference in separate notes.

A low electrical hum or buzz needs a connection check rather than a treble-limiting experiment.
