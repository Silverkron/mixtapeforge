Noise reduction

Dolby HX Pro Explained: Why It Needs No Playback Decoder

HX Pro improves recording headroom through the deck’s bias system, not through a playback setting.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. What you may hear
  2. How to use a deck that has it
← Back to articles
Quick answer

HX Pro helps a recorder handle strong high-frequency music. It changes bias, the ultrasonic electrical signal used while magnetizing the tape, according to the audio being recorded. It is a recording-side function: there is no HX Pro decoder to enable on the playback deck.

What you may hear

A loud, bright cymbal can lose clarity when tape is driven too hard. HX Pro aims to improve the recorder’s available treble headroom—the space before that limitation becomes objectionable. It does not remove all saturation, and it does not reduce hiss by the encode/decode method used by Dolby B.

A cassette recorded with HX Pro can therefore be played on an ordinary compatible cassette player. If Dolby B was also used, that separate recording still needs B decoding. Read bias, EQ and NR to distinguish the three controls.

How to use a deck that has it

You need the deck’s operating manual, a suitable blank and a bright musical passage. Many decks run HX Pro automatically; do not search for a switch the model does not have.

  1. Check whether the manual describes HX Pro as automatic or switchable.
  2. Set the correct tape type and adjust recording level normally.
  3. Record a short passage with cymbals, stop and replay. Listen for crisp attacks and a natural decay.
  4. If the loud cymbals become dull or splashy, repeat at a lower REC LEVEL. Use the treble-saturation test to investigate further.

This checks the recording result; it does not isolate HX Pro’s contribution unless the deck provides a controlled on/off comparison.

MixtapeForge cannot change the deck’s ultrasonic bias through its ordinary audio output. A tape-simulation plugin’s virtual bias control changes the simulation, not the physical recorder. Keep the source clean and let the deck perform its recording function.
