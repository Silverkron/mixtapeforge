Type I, II and IV Cassette Tapes: What Changes When You Record?

Choose a cassette your deck can record, not merely play.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. Read two labels before buying
  2. What the type does not tell you
← Back to articles
Quick answer

Choose a cassette your deck can record, not merely play. Type I, II and IV describe tape categories that require different recording conditions; they are not quality grades that every recorder can interchange.

Read two labels before buying

Check the cassette shell or box for its type, then the deck manual’s recording specification. Normal usually means Type I, high-bias/chrome Type II and metal Type IV. A playback claim is insufficient: TEAC’s W-1200, for example, plays Type IV but records Type I and II. Old Type III ferrichrome needs specific support and is a poor first experiment.

  1. For a first recording, choose a known-good Type I explicitly supported by your deck.
  2. With the deck stopped, insert it and check the detected type or set the manual selector. Do not cover recognition holes or infer type solely from shell colour.
  3. Use the short recording procedure with quiet music and strong vocals/cymbals. Rewind and listen, rather than judging the unused tape by its label.
  4. If trying another formulation, repeat the level test and save separate notes. A setting suitable for one tape may overload another.

What the type does not tell you

Two Type I cassettes can differ in condition and recording sensitivity. Their age alone does not establish whether they work. Playback EQ is the deck’s matching frequency correction; it is separate from Dolby and from the app’s musical EQ. See bias, EQ and noise reduction before changing these controls.

Check: recording support confirmed; type selected correctly; shell runs freely; both channels and loud passages checked. If sound briefly disappears or the transport struggles, investigate the cassette rather than immediately adding treble.
