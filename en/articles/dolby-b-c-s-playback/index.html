Noise reduction

Dolby B, C or S: Which Playback Setting Should You Use?

Choose playback noise reduction from the recording information, then investigate mismatches methodically.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. Prepare the comparison
  2. Interpret what you hear
← Back to articles
Quick answer

The playback NR switch tells the deck which recording process to reverse. It does not select how much hiss you would like removed. The useful question is: how was this particular tape recorded?

Before you start
  • Dolby B Explained: How It Reduces Tape Hiss

Prepare the comparison

You need the cassette, its case or recording notes, a deck with the required NR mode and headphones. Start at moderate volume. Set tape equalization separately: 70/120 µs playback EQ and Dolby are different choices.

  1. Look for “Dolby B”, “C”, “S” or “NR off” on the original label. For your own recording, check the saved recording notes. A logo on the empty cassette shell alone does not document its recording.
  2. Select the documented mode. No NR during recording means NR off during playback.
  3. Play a quiet vocal passage, a strong passage and a fade-out. Listen for natural consonants, stable background and a gradual decay.
  4. If the mode is unknown, compare OFF and the modes available on your deck over the same passage. Keep the listening volume comparable; do not select the mode merely because it is quietest.
  5. Record your choice and whether it is documented or only an estimate. For an irreplaceable unknown tape, retain an unprocessed, NR-off digital capture as well as any listening copy.

Interpret what you hear

Unnaturally bright soft sounds with NR off can suggest an encoded tape; dullness with NR on can suggest a mismatch. Neither proves the format, because worn tape, bad head contact and recording EQ can sound similar. If every mode is poor, use the dull-playback diagnosis.

A deck lacking C or S cannot reconstruct that mode by selecting B. Use matching hardware or a verified software decoder for the actual format. Mark uncertain identifications as uncertain rather than rewriting the cassette’s history.
