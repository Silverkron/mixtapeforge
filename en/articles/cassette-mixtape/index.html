How to Make a Cassette Mixtape: Planning Side A and Side B

A satisfying mixtape has two deliberate beginnings and two endings.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. Prepare the music
  2. Make the result easy to play
← Back to articles
Quick answer

A satisfying mixtape has two deliberate beginnings and two endings. Build the listening journey first, then make it fit the tape; filling every second is optional.

Prepare the music

Gather local audio files, a compatible blank cassette and a working recorder. Start with source files that play correctly. Choose a mood, person or occasion, then select an opening song and an ending for each side. A quiet ending before turning the cassette can feel more natural than cutting off a long song.

  1. In MixtapeForge, add tracks to the Mixtape screen. Drag their handles to reorder or move between A and B; the side panels show their durations.
  2. Listen to the last part of one song followed by the next beginning. Adjust pauses only where the change needs breathing space. Double-click a track to inspect cuts, pauses and gain; avoid trimming an intentional fade.
  3. Check each side’s available time, including pauses. Replace or move a song if the side exceeds its allowance.
  4. If tracks jump in apparent loudness, use normalization deliberately. An album sequence may deserve its original quiet/loud relationships.
  5. Save the project. Follow the recording-from-computer procedure for a short tape test, then both sides.

Make the result easy to play

After listening to the tape, open Cover and check that A/B ordering matches the recording. Add a title and a short dedication. Current built-in cover layouts support up to 15 tracks in the whole project; a longer mix needs a separate layout rather than unreadably small text.

If a transition surprises you, revise the order before rerecording the side. If the recording lacks its first note, check leader clearance rather than changing the playlist.

Finished: both sides fit and play completely, transitions sound intentional, cover matches the tape, playback NR is written down.
