Two-Head vs Three-Head Decks: Recording, Monitoring and Calibration

The useful difference is when you can hear the tape you have just recorded.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. What the heads do
  2. Check what you are actually hearing
← Back to articles
Quick answer

The useful difference is when you can hear the tape you have just recorded. A three-head deck with off-tape monitoring can play it during recording; a conventional two-head deck requires stopping and rewinding.

What the heads do

The erase head removes the previous signal. In a two-head deck, one combined head records or plays; in a three-head deck those functions use separate heads. This allows a playback head to read the moving tape just after recording. It does not make every three-head deck better than every two-head model: condition and alignment still matter.

Check what you are actually hearing

Use headphones connected to the deck, a blank tape and the computer recording setup.

  1. Find the manual’s monitor section. SOURCE/INPUT normally means the incoming signal, before recording. TAPE or a documented AUTO mode can select the recorded signal on a suitable deck.
  2. Record a short familiar passage. On a three-head model, switch between source and tape as its manual instructs. Allow for the slight tape delay; compare tonal balance and distortion, not which sounds louder.
  3. On a two-head model, stop, rewind and play the same passage. Sound heard during recording may only be the input. The TASCAM manual explicitly distinguishes the 122MKIII’s off-tape monitoring from the 112MKII’s input monitoring.
  4. If the tape is poorer, repeat the level test before adjusting anything inside the machine.

Check: monitor source identified; actual tape heard; playback NR matches recording; comparison at similar listening volume. MixtapeForge cannot add a physical playback head. Its calibration capture workflow can instead analyse a recorded test after rewinding.
