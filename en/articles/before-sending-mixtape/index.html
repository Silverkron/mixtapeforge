Troubleshooting

Before You Send a Mixtape: A Recording and Packaging Checklist

Check the actual tape, labels and case so the next listener can press play with confidence.

MixtapeForge editorial teamUpdated November 1, 20262 min read
In this guide
  1. What you need
  2. Finish the package
← Back to articles
Quick answer

The project may be perfect while the physical cassette has a missing opening, an interrupted song or the wrong playback label. Check the finished tape before giving it away. This is a listening inspection, not a laboratory calibration.

Before you start
  • Recording a Mixtape for Someone Else: Playback Compatibility

What you need

Have the finished cassette, its tracklist, headphones and the intended player if available. Keep the digital master/project until the recipient has a working copy.

  1. Rewind side A and play from the beginning. Confirm the first notes are present after the clear leader—the unrecordable tape at the start—and that the case identifies side A correctly.
  2. Listen through the side at normal volume. Check transitions, accidental computer sounds, sudden level changes and brief dropouts. Skipping only to a few tracks cannot certify the whole side.
  3. Confirm the final track ends fully before the leader or auto-stop. Repeat the same inspection for side B. The planning duration is not a substitute for listening to the actual ending.
  4. If available, play a quiet passage and a strong passage on the recipient’s player. Confirm the required NR mode exists and use it. Follow compatibility checks if the result changes.
  5. Inspect the cassette shell and tape opening for loose labels, slack or damage. Do not use a visibly damaged cassette as a gift copy.
  6. Write the title, A/B track order and exact NR mode, including “NR off”, on the insert. Add tape EQ information if the recording uses an unusual setting.

Finish the package

After the recording is final, protect it from accidental recording with the shell’s write-protection arrangement, following its instructions. Put it in a rigid case and keep it away from heat and strong magnetic fields during storage and transport. Do not attach a label where it can obstruct hubs or the tape opening.

The final pass is complete when both sides have been heard, beginnings and endings are intact, and another person can choose the correct playback setting from the notes. A short personal note can then focus on the music instead of explaining a preventable technical problem.
